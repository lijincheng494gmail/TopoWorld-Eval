

\begin{abstract}
Multimodal models can answer spatial questions without producing a coherent
executable world. We introduce \tw{}, an evaluation of whether models
construct and maintain grounded, geometrically consistent, and revisable
worlds from multiple architectural drawings. It contains 14 buildings,
42 drawing pages, 95 determinate relations, and one unresolved identity
relation. We compare direct answering, independent world construction, and
construction assisted by correct relations. Across eight GPT product
configurations, the 12-building point-anchored condition yields 98.17\%
answer accuracy and 88.50\% world-readout accuracy. Among jointly
contract-valid opportunities, answering alone is correct in 34 cases and
construction alone in five. Correct relations rescue 29 construction errors
but introduce 11. Qwen largely aligns answering and construction in N2,
while complex stairs expose interface failures. We also measure grounding,
material consistency, and evidence withdrawal. Spatial answering,
executable construction, and world maintenance require separate measurements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Our question is: \emph{Can multimodal models maintain a grounded,
coherent, and revisable 3D world across multiple views and tasks?}
A spatial assistant should refer to the same building when it answers,
constructs, and revises. A stair seen in a plan must match the intended
stair in a section. Its generated endpoints must meet the right floor
surfaces. A window must keep its host across views. When a new section
resolves a height ambiguity, the model should update that fact while
preserving other supported relations.

These requirements are related, but they are different tests. A correct
sentence about a staircase does not establish an executable connection.
A geometrically consistent program can describe the wrong building.
Even a world that satisfies every fixed query may contain a conflict in
additional generated material (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}).
