\tw{} connects these measurements on the same architectural evidence.
For fixed targets, route R answers relations directly; route A constructs
an executable world independently; route B constructs from the same
evidence plus correct relation values. A deterministic reader derives
answers from the generated geometry and instance bindings. It does not
read route R. We separately check the registered target fragment and
everything the model additionally declares.

Our bounded interface supports rooms, hosted openings, walls, slabs,
straight stair runs, and assemblies with landings. It permits multiple
geometric completions and separates invalid syntax, wrong bindings,
incompatible interfaces, material obstructions, and uncertainty.

The experiments cover eight GPT product configurations at recorded
\texttt{xhigh} effort and a fixed Qwen snapshot. The main 12-building N2
condition gives anonymous target points; the two-building CS1 supplement
also specifies the target unit, such as a flight or assembly. These are
given-target tasks, not full-page object discovery. The two providers use
different interfaces, so the comparison concerns declared workflows rather
than isolated neural weights.

The experiments measure when these observable outputs align across
input conditions and provider workflows. Correct relations rescue some
construction errors and introduce others. Explicit knowledge updates can
leave retained spatial relations changed. R and A use independent
contexts, so their difference concerns task performance rather than
direct evidence of a shared internal state.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) We introduce a benchmark that links source-grounded relation
answering, executable construction, and evidence revision through
shared multi-view building evidence.
(2) We define separate measures of relation realization, registered and
all-declared geometry, material obstruction, image grounding, and evidence
retention through a typed world interface and deterministic readouts.
(3) We provide an empirical analysis of preserved first outputs across
model configurations, input conditions, and two provider workflows,
including successful alignment, assisted rescues and regressions,
contract failures, and measurement limitations.
