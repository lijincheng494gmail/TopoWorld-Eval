\section{Main Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}



\subsection{Configurations and first-output accounting}
The eight GPT configurations are GPT-6 Astra; GPT-6.1 Sol and Terra;
GPT-6 Sol and Luna; and GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna.
These are product-visible aliases within one provider family; an immutable
backend weight revision is not exposed. Codex permits reading assigned
files and images, cropping those pixels, and saving the first response.
The protocol excludes hidden references, other model outputs, solvers,
checker feedback, search, and delegated solving.

The preserved campaign has 688 task stages: 288 historical v1 R/A/B,
288 N2, 48 CS1, and 64 revision stages. Of the stages, 651 satisfy
the output contract.
Standard raw paths contain 684 files, one empty. Missing, malformed,
over-limit, and wrong-location outputs are retained. Observed access
departures and technical recovery diagnostics remain separate from
primary scores (supplement).

\subsection{Answering and construction differ}
In N2, R achieves 589/600 and A 531/600.
On the 570 opportunities where both contracts are valid,
R alone is correct in 34 cases and A alone in five.
The paired difference $A-R$ is $-5.09$ percentage points.
Thus the difference is not explained solely by JSON or delivery failure.
A readout may still fail through geometry, an invalid interface, or
uncertainty; it is not automatically a hidden visual error.

An exploratory building-cluster bootstrap uses 20,000 resamples,
retaining all configurations and queries of each sampled N2 building.
The 95\% interval for $A-R$ is $[-9.29,-1.55]$ points.
It describes variation over these 12 buildings, not unseen model or
deduplicated layout families.

\subsection{Correct relations help and hurt}
Within 533 jointly valid A/B opportunities, B rescues 29 errors and
introduces 11: a net increase of 18, or 3.38 points, with an exploratory
cluster interval $[0.19,6.42]$.
On all 600 opportunities, B improves by only six correct readouts.
Unequal contract-failure sets affect the allocated comparison. The
common-valid comparison isolates content changes within a shared subset.

CS1 has 15 R-only and two A-only successes in 140 common valid
opportunities. B rescues 15 and damages five.
With only two buildings, we report these counts without a stable
population confidence claim.

\subsection{Reading the high answer scores}
N2 has 61 TRUE and 14 FALSE references, so an always-TRUE answer reaches
81.33\%. CS1 has ten of each, giving 50\%.
These controls describe answer-label imbalance, not world construction.
The supplement gives per-configuration and per-building outcomes;
eight configurations are not eight independent model families.
