\section{Executable Worlds and Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{A bounded executable interface}
We summarize the implemented world as
\begin{equation}
W=(\mathcal O,\mathcal G,\alpha,\mathcal R,\mathcal P).
\end{equation}
\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{.77\columnwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Field & Executable meaning\\ \midrule
$\mathcal O$ & Physical instances, parts, assemblies, interfaces\\
$\mathcal G$ & Occupied or free regions in a shared coordinate frame\\
$\alpha$ & Observation-to-instance bindings, including unresolved bindings\\
$\mathcal R$ & Typed declarations: hosts, floors, identity, connections\\
$\mathcal P$ & Sources, active status, and explicit support edges\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{World fields connect physical identity, executable geometry,
and evidence support in one inspectable artifact.}
\label{tab:world}
\end{table}

Boxes and box unions represent material, free space, openings, or interfaces.
Straight stair runs expand into tread geometry; finite assemblies declare
runs and landings. The assembly material must equal its parts' material
union. Transforms are restricted to supported signed-axis permutations
and translations. Unknown geometry is explicit.

Rooms represent spatial regions, not solid obstacles. Walls, slabs, and
treads occupy material. Hosted openings remove material rather than merely
overlaying a rectangle. A stair's connection labels do not prove contact:
its endpoint interfaces, landing top surfaces, host spaces, and run chain
must satisfy the corresponding geometric tests. Observation identity is
read from $\alpha$ and compared with the source-grounded reference.

\subsection{Separate measurements}
\textbf{Representation validity} distinguishes delivery, character/entity
limits, JSON, Schema, and compilation. The frozen output budget is
42,000 characters and 60 entities; first outputs are preserved without
repair. Unsupported or unknown geometry is not counted as a definite
physical error.

\textbf{Relation realization} compares direct R answers or deterministic
world readouts with the fixed reference queries. For each route,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Acc} =
\frac{\#\,\text{correct determinate readouts}}
     {\#\,\text{allocated determinate opportunities}}.
\end{equation}
Missing and contract-invalid outputs remain in this denominator.
Reference-unknown identity is evaluated separately for appropriate
uncertainty. A definite answer there is unsupported certainty, not
necessarily a provably false physical claim.

\textbf{Geometry coherence} has two scopes. Registered geometry checks
geometric target roles and their dependency closure. All-declared geometry also
checks additional generated structures. Material-obstruction tests
identify declared material blocking supported tread/interface regions;
they do not certify full body clearance. For scope $\Gamma$, joint success is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:joint}
\begin{aligned}
J_\Gamma=\ind[&V\land C\\
 &\land(\forall q\in K:\widehat y_q=y_q)\\
 &\land\mathrm{Geom}_\Gamma(W)=\mathrm{PASS}],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is contract validity, $C$ required coverage, and $K$ the
determinate queries. We also report each component, because one scalar
cannot distinguish missing geometry from an obstruction.

\textbf{Grounding and revision} are separate. Given-point localization
uses observation boxes and exact source IDs at IoU$\geq0.25$ against
approximate reference boxes. Revision checks explicit measured knowledge,
active sources and dependencies, and retained spatial readouts under
current evidence.

\subsection{Instrument qualification}
N2 and CS1 prospectively use evaluator 2.2.1. Qualification includes 83
unit tests, 29 independent re-encoding/counterexample checks, and a
200-case blind-hole diagnostic. These are software checks, not building
samples. Historical v1 was generated under 2.2 and uniformly reassessed;
its score change is not model improvement. Original outputs and scores
remain intact. A later slab-encoding limitation is reported with the
results rather than selectively changing one model's score.
